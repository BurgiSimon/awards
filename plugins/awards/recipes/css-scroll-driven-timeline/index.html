How the harbour keeps time with the sea.

A short read in five parts. The bar along the top, the paragraphs as they arrive and the trace that opens halfway down are CSS scroll timelines; the script on this page only reports which engine ran.

The well

A tide gauge starts with a pipe sunk into the quay. Water enters through a narrow slot near the bottom, so the chop of the waves cancels out and only the slow rise of the tide reaches the float inside.

The float

A copper float rides that still water and turns a drum on a fine chain. Twice a day the drum winds one way, and twice a day it winds back.

The trace

The table

From years of traces the harbour office prints a table of predicted heights. It holds to a few centimetres, unless a long onshore wind piles the water against the coast.

The read

Reading the table takes one rule: find the day, find the hour, and add the height to the chart datum printed on the cover.
