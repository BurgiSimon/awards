Lit at the tip, gone by the last line.

Scroll through the stage below. A resin cone, turned in code from a lathe profile, wears a stock standard material patched with a noise threshold. As the scroll advances, everything under the threshold is discarded from the tip down, and a thin ember band burns where the cone is about to go. Scroll back and it re-forms.

Cone No. 7 · cedar, benzoin, clove

  1. Pressed by hand.

    Cedar, benzoin and a pinch of clove, ground with charcoal and shaped into a cone six centimetres tall. It stands on a glazed dish and asks for one match.

  2. It burns from the tip.

    The ember moves down the cone at about a centimetre every eight minutes. The grain of the resin decides the edge, so it is never a straight line and never the same twice.

  3. What stays is the room.

    Forty minutes later the cone is gone and the dish is clean. The scent has only started: it sits in the curtains until the evening.

The threshold is the scroll.

Nothing on this page is a video or a sequence of frames. One uniform, uProgress, is compared with a noise field that sticks to the cone and leans along the world's vertical axis. Scroll back up and the cone re-forms, grain for grain.
