Site diary

The 1891 baths on Marrow Lane are being restored so people can swim in them again. We write from the site every week or so. Entries you have not read carry a New mark, and the count follows you up to the menu.

  1. 24 Oct 2026

    Week 41 · Roof

    The roof lantern goes back on, New

    Thirty-two panes cut to the 1891 drawings went up over the main pool hall in one dry morning.

    Read the entryClose the entry: the roof lantern

    The lantern frame came back from the foundry in August, blasted and primed; the glass waited for a day without wind. On Tuesday the crane lifted it in four sections and the glaziers had the last pane bedded by two.

    For the first time since the 1970s the pool hall is lit from above. The light falls exactly where the old photographs show it, on the deep end.

  2. 17 Oct 2026

    Week 40 · Finishes

    Tiles from the reclamation yard match at last, New

    After four kiln batches that were nearly right, a yard in the north turned up 600 glazed green tiles from a demolished baths.

    Read the entryClose the entry: the tiles

    The surviving dado is a deep bottle green with a crackle that new glazes never quite reproduce. The reclaimed tiles are the same size to the millimetre, which suggests the same maker.

    They go on the gallery stair first, where the most original tiles were lost; the new kiln batch fills the plant room, where nobody will compare.

    Works complete 71 %

  3. 9 Oct 2026

    Week 39 · Structure

    The boiler-house chimney is safe to climb, New

    Scaffold now runs the full 28 metres and the masons have repointed the top third.

    Read the entryClose the entry: the chimney

    The engineer's survey found the top six courses loose and the cap cracked through. Both have been rebuilt in matching brick, and the lightning conductor, missing since the war, is back.

    The chimney stays as a landmark only: the new heat pumps sit in the old coal store and need no flue.

    Works complete 68 %

  4. 30 Sep 2026

    Week 38 · Neighbours

    Open day: 214 people in hard hats, New

    Two of them had learnt to swim in the small pool in 1958 and showed us where the steps used to be.

    Read the entryClose the entry: the open day

    Tours ran every half hour from ten. The most asked question was when the slipper baths will reopen; the answer is with the small pool, next summer.

    The steps our two visitors remembered were found under a later floor the following week.

  5. 19 Sep 2026

    Week 36 · Archaeology

    Slipper baths found under the floor, New

    Six cast-iron tubs, still plumbed, sealed under a 1960s screed in the old ladies' wing.

    Read the entryClose the entry: the slipper baths

    Before houses on the lane had bathrooms, a slipper bath cost a penny and came with a towel. Ours were simply floored over when the last of them closed.

    Two will be restored and shown as they were; four go to the borough's museum store.

    Works complete 63 %

  6. 12 Sep 2026

    Week 35 · Pool

    The pool tank holds water for the first time in forty years, New

    A seven-day test fill lost less than two centimetres, all of it to evaporation.

    Read the entryClose the entry: the test fill

    The tank was relined in the spring. Filling it took most of a weekend from a single hydrant, and the street came to watch the level rise past the old depth markings.

    It has been drained again so the tilers can start; the next time it fills, it stays full.

    Steel for the gallery arrives, New

    Eleven beams on two lorries, craned in over the lane before the school run.

    Read the entryClose the entry: the gallery steel

    The spectators' gallery was taken down in 1979 as unsafe. The new one follows its line exactly, in steel this time, with the original iron brackets kept as ornament.

    Lifting started at six and the lane reopened at eight fifteen.

The building

Public baths, opened 1891, closed 1984: a main pool, a learner pool, slipper baths and a laundry, built by the borough for streets that had no bathrooms.

Visit

The site is closed while work goes on. Tours run on the last Saturday of each month and are announced here first.
