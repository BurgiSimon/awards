How the tray behaves

How the tray behaves

  1. Near you, pushed; far away, untouched. Inside a circle around the pointer each button is pushed straight away from it, turned and lifted a little, by a weight that falls from 1 at the pointer to 0 at the circle's edge. Outside the circle nothing is written at all.
  2. Home on a spring. Pushed buttons follow quickly and stop without a wobble; released, they swing back past their places once or twice and settle. The springs step at a fixed rate, so a fast screen and a slow one feel the same.
  3. Still when it is done. Places are read once and again only when the layout changes. Once every button has settled, the tray stops asking for frames until the pointer moves again.
  4. Not only for a mouse. A finger parts the tray where it lands and drags the circle sideways; a swipe up or down still scrolls the page and lets the tray go. The nudge button sends one wave out from the centre, so a keyboard reaches the toy too, and Escape stills the tray at once. With reduced motion the tray holds still, the nudge is hidden and every touch scrolls the page.
