Hatchmoor makes short films by hand, one frame at a time.

Paper, wire, felt and patience, in a converted chapel on the edge of the moor. Point at a name or tab to it and the label turns over like a small block to show its other side; on a touch screen, a press fills it in.

Eleven minutes, paper and tin. A keeper lights a lamp for a ship that never comes.

Moth Season

Paper Harbour

Fourteen minutes, folded newsprint. A harbour town that rebuilds itself every tide.

Seven Small Winters

Nine minutes, wool and salt. A year on a hill farm, told by the dog.

The Last Kiln

Twelve minutes, clay that was really fired. The final firing of a village pottery.

Studio

Four animators, one cinematographer and a model maker who used to build church organs. We shoot on a rostrum camera at twelve frames a second and build every set at the scale of a hand.

The labels on this page are the studio's habit made into an interface: a block with a word on each face, turned to show the next one. The real name of each link is the word you see first; the face that turns in is decoration and is hidden from screen readers.

Journal

Notes from the bench: how long a felt moth takes to make (two days), why paper harbours need a humid room, and what a lantern looks like at one frame a second.
