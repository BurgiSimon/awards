Eight places on a small island

Skerrow is two kilometres of turf and shingle, reached by one small ferry. Point at a pin, tab to it or pick a place from the list: each one opens a card with the single number worth knowing before you walk there.

The island, pinned

  1. North Well

    8 °C all year

    A spring under a corbelled stone hood, still the island's drinking water.

    Refill points

  2. Skerrow Head light

    31 m to the lantern

    Climb its 132 steps on summer weekends; the keeper's log lies open on the landing.

    Climbing times

  3. The Sisters

    4,200 years standing

    Five stones in a ring on the hill's shoulder, the tallest set on the midwinter sunset.

    Read the survey

  4. Marsh hide

    212 species logged since 1998

    A turf-roofed hide over the saltings at the head of the loch: wigeon in winter, terns from May.

    Recent sightings

  5. West Cove bothy

    6 bunks no booking

    An unlocked stone shelter above the cove, first come, first served, a stove and no tap.

    The bothy code

  6. Kelp Store

    38 seats and a stove

    Where weed was once burnt for soda ash; now a café and a room of the island's photographs.

    Opening hours

  7. Shell Beach

    2.4 km of shingle

    Crushed shell rather than sand, which is why it rings like glass underfoot.

    Tide tables

  8. Ferry slip

    40 min between sailings

    The only landing. The last boat leaves an hour before high water, whatever the timetable says.

    Sailing times

Before you cross

The ferry runs from Ardnish pier every forty minutes from eight until an hour before high water. There is one tap, at the Kelp Store, and the North Well beside the track; the bothy has neither.

Every link on the cards comes here in this demo. On a real site each would open its own page: the timetable, the survey, the sightings log.
