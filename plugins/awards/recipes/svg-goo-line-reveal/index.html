svg-goo-line-reveal

Letters gather out of a pool of ink.

Each line of a heading gets its own SVG filter: a Gaussian blur pulled down to nothing while an alpha threshold relaxes back to identity, so the letters condense out of a dark pool and land crisp. The filter comes off as each line lands. The journal and all its copy are a synthetic demo.

Every line owns its filter, so the second can still be pooling while the first has already set.

The lines start a beat apart. The blur eases out over a little more than a second, and the threshold lets go only in its last half second, which is when the edges soften back into ordinary type.

When the last line lands, the filters and the split are gone and the heading is plain text again.

Nothing is left on the text after the reveal: no filter for the compositor to keep, no line wrappers for a screen reader to step through, nothing between a reader's cursor and the words.

Under reduced motion the heading only fades in.

No split, no blur, no threshold at any moment: a short fade once the heading reaches the viewport, and with motion off altogether, the text is simply there.
