## 1. Concept and narrative
The proposition is *access to an impossible place, rendered as logistics*. Awe is delivered in measurements rather than adjectives: camp coordinates set in brackets under each card, a flight chapter labelled "CPT – WFR" carrying flying time 05:30 hrs, distance 4,220 km / 2,610 mi and average summer temperature -5°C / 23°F [verified, index.html + desktop-s50.png]. Beats read, in order [verified, index.html spine + desktop-s00/25/50/75/100.png]: full-bleed film hero with the wordmark "Antarctica" set edge to edge and one italic serif line, "Luxury and adventure in the most remote place on Earth"; an intro chapter (The Last Continent / Our Season); five priced trips from US $16,500 to US $115,500 as flickable cards; a signed founder statement from the co-founder & CEO; three camps, each with its own concept sentence and coordinates — "Inspired by astronauts, used by explorers" for one, "a side of Antarctica seldom seen" for another; a full-bleed press pull-quote over a crevasse; the Cape Town → Wolf's Fang flight on a sticky globe; a six-step "How it works" flyout; a dense operational footer closing on Shackleton's "We all have our own white South".

The distinctive move is that **price and process are on the homepage**. This is a high-ticket enquiry site that refuses to hide its numbers — the persuasion is that the operation is real, and the proof is that it will quote you before you ask.

## 7. Principles
1. **Specificity is the luxury signal.** Coordinates, flight time, distance and temperature in dual units do more persuading than any adjective, and they are cheap to author.
2. **Publish the price when the product is unbelievable.** On a high-ticket enquiry site, a visible number converts scepticism into logistics; withholding it reads as evasion.
3. **Reserve one saturated accent for the conversion path only.** A two-colour brand pair plus a single functional orange keeps the page calm and makes the CTA unmissable without a size war.
4. **Invert the type habit.** Let the grotesque take the giant sizes and the editorial serif live in italic at reading scale — the voice lands in the sentences, not the billboard.
5. **Show the layout system.** A faint column grid drawn over full-bleed photography frames the images and signals rigour rather than decoration.
6. **Give each property its own concept sentence.** One line of distinct character per camp, trip or SKU beats a shared template, and it is the cheapest differentiation in the build.
