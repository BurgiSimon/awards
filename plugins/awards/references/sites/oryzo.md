## 1. Concept and narrative
- **The one idea:** take a deliberately ridiculous object and present it with keynote sincerity. Lusion's own framing: the product was "so mundane that treating it seriously was already funny" [recalled high — BTS part 1 via search]. The comedy lives in the gap between what the thing is and how seriously it is presented; the site never winks until the last screen.
- **Beats, in served order** [verified, index.html]: preloader → hero (macro coaster on a cutting mat, founder video card) → **AI** (a hover-a-hand interaction whose punchline is a six-fingered AI hand, footnoted "* Adobe Illustrator") → wearable (a rail of product-on-body shots plus a fake magazine spread and an in-page AI prompt bar) → features (elevate / thermodynamic stability / a drawn curve) → encryption ("smart flip encryption") → grip (the extreme-zoom section) → sustainability (two Rive scenes) → testimonies (a review table) → social content (a nine-item rail) → product (ORYZO / Pro / Pro Max) → open weight (the fake paper: Abstract, Acknowledgements, Peer Review, BibTeX) → footer.
- **Tone:** deadpan technical-marketing register played straight, then broken exactly once, in the footer: "We caught your attention with a non-existent product. If we can sell a coaster, imagine what we can do for your brand." [verified, index.html] — with the legal-safety line beneath it, "This entire site is a fictional creative project by Lusion. Oryzo doesn't exist." [verified, same].
- **The world beyond the page:** an "open weight" GitHub release, a founder video, social content [recalled high; the repo verified]. The GitHub artefact is the sharpest writing: six OBJ checkpoints named like LLM releases (base 26b / 40b / 108b / 145b, instruct 168b, frontier 344b), a fabricated eval suite "WoodenBench", limitations citing "heavy dependency on gravity, mugs, and human deployment", a `paper.pdf` and an MIT licence [verified — lusionltd/ORYZO-1].
- **Awwwards' citation:** "A cinematic product story that turns an ordinary cork coaster into an immersive digital experience through craft, detail, and interaction." [verified, Awwwards entry].

## 3. Visual language
- **Palette roles:** warm near-black `#100904` ground, cream `#ffedd7` ink, olive greens `#445231` / `#5d6c49` for the cutting mat and the sustainability chapter, browns `#382416` / `#6c5f51` for pills and secondary copy, `#dc5000` orange for accents, glows and the send/submit affordances [verified]. No pure black as ground — the same warm-shifted neutral logic as [site:lama-lama], [site:floema] and [site:shopify-editions-w26].
- **Type scale, artboard-locked** [verified, CSS]: `--screen-unit: min((100vw − 2×padding)/(1920 − 120), (vh×100)/1024 × 1.25)`, so every size is a **1920 × 1024** artboard number — h1 164, h2 68, h3 45, h4 32, h5 20; body 38 / 24 / 18; quote 30; sub 24 / 16. The phone re-declares the same tokens against a **375** artboard: h1 96, h2 38, body 18 / 16 / 14.
- **Grid** [verified, CSS]: 16 columns desktop → 6 → 4 on phone; `--site-padding-x: 3.125vw` desktop, `16/375` of the viewport on phone; the gap is a ratio (`24/90` of a column), so gutters scale with the column rather than with a fixed px.
- **Materials:** the hero reads expensive through cork grain, depth of field and a single warm key light on one mesh, not through an elaborate environment [verified, desktop-s00.png / desktop-rm-s00.png].
- **Illustration** is a major register beside the 3D [recalled high]. AI was allowed into parts of the pipeline without being allowed to define the final look — a boundary the studio states explicitly [recalled high]; the site then makes that boundary the joke, with a six-fingered AI hand footnoted to Illustrator [verified, index.html].
- **Art-direction rule (in Lusion's words):** the loud parts — the desk scene, the humour, the illustrations — "only work because everything around them stays relatively quiet" [recalled high]. Two of the five desktop frames are almost empty, which is that rule made visible [verified, desktop-s50.png].
- **Browser surfaces:** `theme-color` is `#000000`, not the site's `#100904` ground — the one surface token that does not match the system [verified, index.html]. `color-scheme` is not declared [verified].

## 4. Motion and effects (with parameters)
- **Libraries:** the patched Three.js r178 does the motion; **GSAP SplitText 3.14.2 is the only GSAP code served**, used for the footer scroll label [verified]. No ScrollTrigger, no Lenis, no Locomotive, no anime.js, no page-transition library [verified — all absent from the served bundle].
- **Scroll engine** [verified, hoisted.CRsATKbF.js]: wheel, touch and drag feed a velocity integrator with friction coefficients (`frictionCoeffFrom = 2.1`, a `dragHistory` window of 0.1 s) and a cubic-Bézier ease inside `ScrollDomRange`; the result is written to the real document each frame and re-read from a passive `scroll` listener. The canvas stays `fixed`; DOM tethering happens by mapping element rects into the scroll timeline, not by moving the canvas.
- **Post-processing** [verified]: SMAA (gated off one Safari build via a user-agent check), FXAA and TAA paths, bloom with a `lens_dirt.jpg`, bokeh (`POST_PROFILE = { bloomAmount: 0, bokehAmount: 1 }` on the coffee scene), AgX and ACES Filmic tone-mapping, blue-noise dither (`LDR_RGB1_0.png`), a BRDF LUT.
- **Gaussian splats** [verified]: the desk props are a `.sog` splat set, depth-sorted in a WASM worker, uploaded as `splatTexture` + `splatIndexTexture` and drawn with `renderOrder = -2` under the meshes.
- **Rive** [verified]: two artboards from `/rive/oryzo.riv` in the sustainability chapter — a harvesting animation and an animated text block — rendered through Rive's advanced canvas renderer with a WASM fallback binary.
- **Pointer** [verified]: an `easedMouseDynamics` input object feeds the sketches and hand interactions; `cursor: grab` on the draggable product surface; a custom cursor only inside the video overlay.
- **Hero physics:** momentum on drag and lighting that responds to the object's motion [recalled high]; numeric damping, mass and light parameters [unknown].
- **Sound:** none observed — the only `AudioContext` references are Rive's WASM audio glue, and no audio file is referenced [verified].
- **Transitions:** Awwwards indexes five separate transition moments (intro, reveal, gallery, 3d-2d-3d) [verified, entry]; their easings and durations [unknown] — they live in the GL scenes, not in CSS.
- **CSS motion, for scale:** exactly one `cubic-bezier(.3, 0, .66, -.3)` in the whole stylesheet and a handful of 0.15–0.5 s opacity/transform transitions [verified, CSS]. Everything that matters is drawn, not tweened.

## 6. Weaknesses
- **Reduced motion: fail.** `prefers-reduced-motion` appears **zero times** in the served CSS and zero times in the served JS, and the reduced-motion captures render the identical six canvases and the full WebGL page [verified, index.TL6TuoJb.css + hoisted.CRsATKbF.js + desktop-rm-s00/50.png + manifest.json]. The earlier `[unknown]` on this card is now a verified gap.
- **Keyboard: partial.** One `keydown` listener exists, on `documentElement`: ArrowUp/ArrowDown move ±100 px and PageUp/PageDown move ±one viewport through the custom scroll engine [verified]. There is no Escape for the video overlay, no keyboard path for the drag, the zoom box or the gallery rail, and no skip link.
- **DOM behind the canvas: pass.** Every headline, spec, review and paper section is real markup; the GL layer decorates text rather than replacing it [verified, index.html]. The exception is the MSDF atlas, which draws some type inside WebGL.
- **Images: fail.** 18 `<img>` elements, **one** carries an `alt` attribute [verified, index.html]. Awwwards scored Accessibility **7.00**, the lowest developer sub-score on this entry and the lowest in the corpus [verified, entry].
- **Landmarks: thin.** `header`, `nav` and `footer` exist; there is no `<main>` and not one `<section>` element — the fourteen chapters are `<div class="section">` [verified, index.html]. Markup/Meta-data scored 7.20, the second-lowest sub-score.
- **Pinch-zoom disabled** by `user-scalable=no` [verified] — a WCAG 1.4.4 problem the award scoring did not dock.
- **One 1.05 MB entry chunk** blocks the whole experience; nothing routes or splits except the splat worker [verified].
- **Usability 7.51 against Creativity 8.35** [verified, entry]: the gap this site trades for on purpose. The newsletter form is the one place where the accessibility work is complete — `aria-labelledby`, `aria-describedby`, `aria-live="polite"`, `aria-required` [verified, index.html] — which shows the gaps elsewhere are budget, not ignorance.
- **What the awards skills do differently:** `/awards:motion` gives the intro interaction a keyboard trigger and, under `prefers-reduced-motion`, completes it instantly — the reduced tier shows a static hero render, no splats, no dwell gate; `/awards:webgl` keeps the DOM mirror and adds `alt` on every product image, canvas `aria-hidden`, a poster fallback when GL is unavailable, and the load gates (GL chunk lazy ≤ 500 KB gz, poster LCP ≤ 2.5 s); `/awards:structure` wraps the chapters in `<main>` and real `<section>` elements and never hides required content behind an easter egg; `/awards:ship` restores pinch-zoom and walks the video overlay, the rail and the zoom box by keyboard.

## 7. Principles (3–6, generalisable)
1. **Commit to a premise with total sincerity, then break it exactly once.** The craft is the punchline; the single wink belongs in the footer, where it converts.
2. **Extreme reduction buys two or three loud moments.** One superfamily, a warm near-black ground and one hot accent — so the desk scene, the illustrations and the closing particle field can be maximal.
3. **One object rendered with real weight beats a whole world.** Inertia, lighting and material response on a single hero mesh read as more expensive than a sprawling scene, at a fraction of the cost.
4. **Lock the type scale to an artboard, not to the viewport.** A `min()` screen-unit against a named 1920 × 1024 canvas keeps a design-tool composition intact at every width and re-declares cleanly for the phone.
5. **Build the world, not just the page.** A fake model card, benchmark, paper and founder film make the site an artefact of something real.
6. **Own the scroll clock without stealing the scrollbar.** Integrate wheel and touch yourself for feel, then write the result back to the real document so anchors, the scrollbar and the keyboard still work.

## 8. Take / Don't take
- **Take:**
  - Straight-faced genre adoption as a concept method: pick a category's complete conventions (keynote launch, model card, benchmark table, peer review) and apply them to your subject with zero tonal breaks until the close.
  - The artboard-locked scale: one `--screen-unit` from `min(width-ratio, height-ratio)`, every size expressed as its design-tool number, the whole set re-declared once for the phone.
  - A ratio-based grid where the gutter is a fraction of the column (`24/90`) rather than a fixed px, so 16 → 6 → 4 columns stay proportionally identical.
  - The scroll architecture: your own inertia integrator, written back with `window.scrollTo({behavior:"instant"})` and re-read from a passive `scroll` listener, with a `fixed` canvas tethered by DOM rects. It keeps native anchors and keyboard scrolling alive, which a transform-based virtual scroll throws away.
  - One WebGL context for the page, with canvas-2D and Rive layers beside it for the things that do not need 3D — the context budget is spent once, deliberately.
  - Chapter wayfinding that costs almost nothing: an underlined nav item driven by `data-id`, plus a fixed vertical pill naming the current chapter.
  - A dwell-gated easter egg inside a zoom section, and an authored closing beat so the last screen is as designed as the first.
- **Don't take:**
  - The coaster, the six-fingered-hand gag, WoodenBench, the checkpoint naming (26b … 344b) or any of the GitHub copy lines.
  - The `#ffedd7` / `#100904` / `#445231` / `#dc5000` set, the Halyard + Literata + DM Mono setting, or the coffee-bean footer as "the Lusion look".
  - The tardigrades, the desk scene, or the fourteen-beat order as a skin.
  - Lusion's illustrations, 3D assets, splat captures, Rive files, launch film or the "-zo.ai" naming pattern.
  - The reduced-motion gap, the single `alt`, the missing `<main>` and `user-scalable=no` — those are what the next build beats, not what it copies.
