## 1. Concept and narrative
- The idea [verified, desktop-s00/25/50/75/100.png + home.js]: the animation *engine* is a literal engine. A lens-and-motor assembly is modelled in 3D, and its parts are named after the library's own modules — `module-timer-01…05`, `module-animate-01`, `module-timeline-01/02`, `module-scroll-01`, `module-svg-01`, `module-stagger-01/02`, `module-spring-01`, `module-scope-01`, `module-draggable-01/02`, `module-easing-01`, `module-waapi-01`, `module-engine-01`, `module-renderer-01`, `module-shield-01/02`. Scrolling drives one master timeline that explodes the machine, inspects a part, and closes on a grid of links to the very modules just shown.
- Beats read from the captures [verified]: `s00` a dark instrument — a rainbow-arc dial with an oscilloscope waveform, a dotted easing curve and tick rings, beside the headline "All-in-one animation engine." and `npm i animejs`. `s25` the page inverts to a warm bone ground and the machine becomes a white-on-grey exploded line drawing under "The complete animator's toolbox". `s50` back to dark, the machine solid and toon-shaded, an orange SVG demo playing inside the lens barrel, a syntax-highlighted code panel bottom-right showing `createMotionPath`, `createDrawable` and `morphTo`. `s75` light again, the lens seen head-on with a green drawn coil inside and a "Bundle size 27.13 KB" panel with a twelve-module legend. `s100` the twelve-cell module grid over the dial, then a footer.
- The scrub ruler [verified, desktop-s25/50/75.png, mobile-s50.png]: a dark tick-ruler with a red playhead sits bottom-right (bottom, full width, on the phone) from the second chapter to the last — a timeline readout for the page, borrowed from the library's own playback controls.
- Register [verified, index.html]: engineering-manual, terse. "Break free from browser limitations and animate anything on the web with a single API."; "Keep your bundle size small by only importing the parts you need."
- What the site does **not** do [verified, index.html]: the ink-drop wordmark choreography that ships as a repo example is not on the homepage — the header is a static `anime-js-logo-v4.svg` `<img>`.

## 2. Structure and components
- Page map [verified, index.html]: `header#site-header` (logo, a native `<select>` version switcher 4.0.0 / 3.2.2 / 2.1.0, nav Docs · Easings · Learn · CodePen · GitHub, a red Sponsor button, `#toggle-site-menu`) → `main#home` → `#intro [HEADING]` → `#toolbox [TOOLBOX, .section-light]` → `#features-gallery [FEATURES]` holding eight `.feature-section`s (`intuitive`, `composition`, `scroll`, `staggering`, `svgUtils`, `draggable`, `clockwork`, `responsive`), each with its own `data-demo` and colour class → `#modules [MODULES, .section-light]` → `#sponsors` → `#get-started [GET_STARTED]` → `footer#site-footer` → `div#engine`.
- Sections carry `data-chapter`, `data-label` (`HEADING`, `TOOLBOX`, `FEATURES`, `MODULES`, `SPONSORS`, `GET_STARTED`) and `lvh` enter/leave offsets; scroll runway is bought with explicit empty `.section-spacer` divs rather than min-heights [verified, index.html].
- Components [verified, captures]: copy-to-clipboard `npm i animejs` chip and a `LEARN MORE ↓` button in the hero; per-chapter code panels with a copy button; the bundle-size stacked bar with a twelve-module legend (Timer, Animation, Timeline, Animatable, Draggable, Scroll, Scope, SVG, Stagger, Spring, WAAPI); a twelve-cell module grid, each cell a coloured dot + label + arrow; a footer with a Carbon ad, sponsor CTA, site and socials columns, and a newsletter field.

## 3. Visual language
- Two grounds, swapped per chapter, not per user setting [verified, home.js + desktop-s25/s75.png]: adding `.is-light` to `<body>` runs a 250 ms `inOutQuad` timeline that tweens the body background **and the shader uniforms together** — `outlineBlend .35`, `contourBlend .35`, `rimColor`, `worldColor1`, `shadowColor`, `backgroundColor` and `outlineColor` all move to the light world tokens. DOM theme and WebGL theme are one tween.
- The accent system is the tell [verified, core.css]: 18 ramps of 8 steps, identical structure, each ramp landing on the tinted ground. Vivid steps as seen: red `#ff4b4b`, corail `#ff7d36`, orange `#ffa828`, yellow `#ffcc2a`, citrus `#f9f640`, lime `#b7ff54`, green `#8dff55`, turquoise `#00ffaa`, cyan `#26f2d5`, sky `#05dbe9`, sega `#33b3f1`, king `#4d9cff`, indigo `#7c85ff`, lavender `#a369ff`, purple `#c06ddf`, magenta `#e962bf`, pink `#ff718b`. Each chapter claims one (`color-turquoise`, `color-svg`, `color-draggable`, `color-timeline`, …), so the arc ring, the heading and the demo agree.
- Geometry tokens [verified, core.css]: margins `--margin-xxxs .25rem` → `--margin-xxxl 4rem`; radii `--br-xs .125rem` → `--br-xl 1rem`; `--visual-viewport-height: 100dvh`; fixed chrome `--site-header-height` 3.5rem → 4.5rem, `--docs-sidebar-width` 14rem → 16rem.
- Imagery [verified, captures]: none. Every picture on the page is geometry — 3D meshes, SVG demos, drawn lines, dot grids and type. No photography, no video, no texture maps observed.
- Layout [verified, captures]: a left text column of roughly a quarter width held against a full-bleed canvas, with instrument panels docked bottom-right. The composition is the same in every chapter; only the machine and the accent change.

## 4. Motion and effects (with parameters)
### The site [verified, home.js + captures]
- One page, one timeline: a master `tl-scroller` timeline with `defaults: { ease: "linear" }` whose `autoplay` is `onScroll({ target: document.body, sync: .9, enter: "max", leave: "min", onUpdate })`. Scroll-linking is an autoplay strategy, not a plugin, and `sync: .9` is the only smoothing on the page — there is no smooth-scroll library.
- Chapters are their own observers: `onScroll({ target: section, container: document.body, enter: "top top" + data-enter-offset, leave: "bottom bottom" + data-leave-offset })`, with offsets like `-=100lvh`, `-=75lvh`, `+=10lvh` authored in the markup. `#features-gallery` gets `sync: .9`, `enter: "top top-=1"`, `leave: "bottom bottom+=100lvh"` and `.link()`s the gallery timeline.
- Chapter reveals are time-based, not scrubbed: cards are pre-set to `{ y: '120%', opacity: 0 }` and shown from `onEnter` / hidden from `onLeave`; the pinned `.fixed-section` fades `opacity 1 ↔ .001` over 350 ms `linear` with `pointerEvents` flipped in the same step.
- Theme swap: 250 ms, `inOutQuad`, DOM + shader uniforms on one timeline (see §3).
- Renderer settings are the performance story [verified, home.js]: `new WebGLRenderer({ powerPreference: 'high-performance', antialias: false, alpha: false, stencil: false, depth: false })` and **`setPixelRatio(1)`** — one device pixel per CSS pixel for everyone, no depth or stencil buffer, outlines and contours done in the shader and the composer instead. There is no device probe, no `matchMedia`, no quality tier anywhere in the entry script.
- Hover and idle are pointer events only (`mouseenter`, `mouseleave`, `pointerenter`, `pointerleave`, `mousemove`); `visibilitychange` is handled so the timeline does not drift in a background tab [verified, home.js].
- SVG set-pieces run inside the 3D lens: a motion-path circuit with an arrowhead, `createDrawable` line drawing, `morphTo` shape swaps and a staggered dot matrix, each in its chapter's accent [verified, desktop-s50/s75/s100.png, mobile-s50.png].

### The library grammar (repo, not observed on the served page)
- Exports and factories [verified, repo]: `animate`, `createTimer`, `createTimeline`, `createAnimatable`, `createDraggable`, `createScope`, `onScroll`; `utils`, `svg`, `text`, `waapi`, `easings`, plus a Three.js adapter. Per-property objects and per-property keyframe arrays, each with its own `duration`, `delay`, `ease`.
- `composition: 'blend'` for additive blending; `stagger(value, { from, start, ease, reversed, modifier, use: 'data-line'|'data-char' })` and range form `stagger([.75, 1])`; timeline positions as numbers, labels or relative strings (`'<<+=380'`, `'SWEECH+=250'`).
- `splitText(target, { chars: { class, clone: 'left'|'right'|'top'|'bottom', wrap: 'clip' } })` writes `data-line` / `data-char` and re-splits on resize via `.addEffect()`; `scrambleText` takes `from`, `chars` presets, `cursor`, `perturbation`, `seed`, `revealDelay`, `revealRate` (default 60/s), `settleDuration` (300 ms), `settleRate` (30/s).
- Easings from one power primitive `easeInPower = (p = 1.68) => t => pow(t, +p)` with `in / out / inOut / outIn` wrappers, so strings compose: `'inOutQuint'`, `'outElastic(1, 1.4)'`, `'in(2)'`, `'steps(10)'`; plus `cubicBezier`, `createSpring`, `irregular`.
- `createScope({ root, defaults, mediaQueries })` with per-query re-execution and teardown; `createAnimatable(target, { modifier })` returning callable setters; `createDraggable(target, { releaseStiffness: 80, velocityMultiplier })`.
- The `animejs-v4-logo-animation` example is one timeline labelled `FALL → WIGGLE → POP → SWEECH → FOUR → TEXT → OUTRO`, with `splashCurve = cubicBezier(0.225, 1, 0.915, 0.980)`, `sweechCurve = outElastic(1.1, 0.9)`, ten sequential `svg.morphTo` path morphs of 60–340 ms, per-axis squash-and-stretch on `stagger(80, { from: 'center' })` letters, four onion-skin clones at `opacity: stagger([1, .4])`, `delay: stagger(18)`, and a `feGaussianBlur` `stdDeviation ['15,15' → '0,0']` over 1000 ms [verified, repo]. **It is not the homepage hero.**

The only failed requests are the two runtime sponsor endpoints and the GA beacon, all aborted at teardown. - Serving [verified]: `Server: Apache`, assets versioned by query string (`?v=1787698626`), OG image generated by the CMS at `/media/pages/home/…/generated-og-image.en.png` 1200 × 600, `<meta name="darkreader-lock">` to stop extensions repainting the themed page.

## 6. Weaknesses
- **Reduced motion: fail** [verified]. There is no `prefers-reduced-motion` query in `core.css`, `home.css` or `home.js` — not one occurrence, and no `matchMedia` call of any kind in the entry script. The reduced-motion captures confirm it: `desktop-rm-s00.png` runs the same dial animation as `desktop-s00.png` at a different phase, so the scene never stopped. Worse, the time-based `onEnter` reveals leave text behind: `desktop-rm-s25.png` shows "The complete animator's toolbox" and its paragraph at roughly 15 % opacity on a ground that has not yet swapped to light, and `desktop-rm-s50.png` shows the next chapter's cyan "SVG toolset" heading painted over the previous chapter's model with its own paragraph still near invisible. Award Animations/Transitions 9.00, Accessibility 7.40 — the gap is exactly here.
- **Keyboard: fail** [verified, home.js]. Not a single `keydown` or `keyup` listener in the entry script; every interaction is `click`, `mouse*` or `pointer*`. The draggable demo has no key equivalent. The version switcher is a real `<select>` and the newsletter a real `<form>`, so the form chrome is fine — the set-pieces are not.
- **DOM behind the canvas: pass** [verified, index.html]. All headings, copy, links and the module grid are real markup; `#engine` ships empty and the page reads without WebGL. The exploded-view labels are CSS2D DOM, not texture.
- **Load gate: pass** — no preloader in any frame; the hero paints with the dial already running [verified, captures].
- **The phone: pass** — designed, not shrunk, and the desktop chapter rhythm survives [verified, mobile-s00/25/50/75/100.png].
- **Wayfinding: partial** [verified, captures]. The scrub ruler shows position within the page but is unlabelled, and no chapter name is ever shown; `data-label` values live in the markup and never reach the screen. The primary action (`npm i animejs`, Docs) is reachable in the first viewport.
- One DPR for everyone [verified, home.js]: `setPixelRatio(1)` keeps a Retina laptop safe and cheap, but the outlines are visibly soft on a high-density display; there is no tier that would let a capable device go to 1.5.
- What the awards skills do differently: a real reduced-motion branch that seeks every chapter timeline to its end state instead of playing it, so no heading is ever sampled at opacity 0; `createScope({ mediaQueries })` around the set-pieces so the branch is one switch; keyboard operation and visible focus for the draggable and the copy chips; a chapter name beside the scrub readout; a quality tier that lifts DPR on a device that can afford it rather than capping everyone at 1.

## 7. Principles (3–6, generalisable)
1. Make the product's architecture the object on screen: if the thing sells modules, model the modules and let the scroll take the machine apart.
2. Theme the render and the document with one tween. A section theme that moves CSS custom properties and shader uniforms on the same 250 ms curve never shows a seam.
3. A ramp rule beats a palette: N hues × N steps where the darkest step is the ground tinted with that hue lets a page carry a different accent per chapter and stay one world.
4. Buy the cheapest renderer that serves the look. Flat toon shading with shader outlines needs no antialias, no depth, no stencil and no DPR above 1 — which is how a full 3D page ships in 60 KB of script.
5. Author scroll ranges in the markup. `data-enter-offset="-=100lvh"` on the section keeps pacing editable by whoever writes the page, not only by whoever wrote the timeline.
6. A number on screen is a claim you can check: publish the bundle size, broken down by module, on the page that asks for the install.

## 8. Take / Don't take
- **Take:**
  - Scroll-linking as an autoplay strategy: `autoplay: onScroll({ enter, leave, sync })`, so one timeline plays on load or on scroll by swapping one option (compare camera scrubbing with a smoothing factor in [site:mont-fort]).
  - One master page timeline at `sync: .9` over native scroll, with per-chapter observers linked into it — smoothing without a smooth-scroll library.
  - Enter/leave offsets as `lvh` data attributes on the section, read by the observer.
  - The 8-step tinted accent ramp: define each hue so its darkest step is the background tinted with that hue, then give each chapter one hue.
  - Theme swap as a tween across both layers: body background plus `rimColor`, `worldColor`, `shadowColor`, `outlineColor` and blend uniforms, 250 ms `inOutQuad`.
  - The lean-renderer recipe: `antialias: false, alpha: false, stencil: false, depth: false`, `setPixelRatio(1)`, outlines in the shader — then spend the budget on geometry.
  - Draco-compressed glTF parts named for the things they represent, so the model and the information architecture share one vocabulary.
  - CSS2D labels with drawn leader lines over a 3D model, re-rendered from the scroll observer's `onUpdate` — annotation that stays selectable and translatable.
  - A page-level scrub readout that doubles as a progress indicator for a scroll-driven timeline.
  - `composition: 'blend'` so hover, scroll and idle on one property coexist; scope + teardown + media queries as the container for framework-embedded motion.
  - The compositional easing grammar (`type(power)` strings from one power primitive) and parametric `outElastic(amplitude, period)` for landings.
  - Split-then-stagger-by-data-attribute so word and line rhythms nest (the GSAP SplitText equivalent is used in [site:lando-norris]); the duplicate-char hover roll (`clone: 'left', wrap: 'clip'`, clone `x: '100%'` at 5 ms per char); onion-skin trails (N clones, offset `transformOrigin`, `opacity: stagger([1, .4])`, `delay: stagger(18)`).
  - The impact morph chain: 4–6 sequential path morphs of 60–140 ms on a landing moment, then a slower 180–340 ms settle; per-axis squash-and-stretch on a wordmark at 190 / 120 / 120 ms up-hold-settle.
  - Persisted tweak panels for showcases; a scramble with separate `revealRate` and `settleRate`.
- **Don't take:**
  - The camera-lens machine as the subject, its exploded-view choreography, or module parts named after your own API.
  - The hue names and hexes (`#252423`, `#ff4b4b`, `#00ffaa`, `#DAD5D0` …) — take the ramp rule, not the values.
  - Berkeley Mono + DINish + Digital-7 as an identity, or "mono for every label" as a look for a non-developer product.
  - The instrument-dial hero, the tick-ruler scrub widget, the bundle-size legend panel or the twelve-cell module grid as compositions.
  - The ink-drop / splash / letters-pop logo choreography and its label names — and do not attribute it to this homepage, which uses a static SVG wordmark.
  - The time-based `onEnter` reveal with no reduced-motion branch: it is the one thing on this page a jury can prove is broken.
