## 1. Concept and narrative
"Every leg of the journey" is the single H1 and the JSON-LD `slogan` [verified, index.html]. The beats read across the capture set: orbit — a dotted point-cloud Earth with orange great-circle trade arcs and country label chips (UK, Germany, Spain, Italy, Egypt, South Africa, Colombia, Brazil, Argentina), blue atmospheric rim, star field [verified, desktop-s00.png, desktop-rm-s00.png]; then a crane setting a container on a truck bed on white [verified, desktop-s25.png]; then the truck seen from directly overhead on a black road strip that splits the white page in two [verified, desktop-s50.png]; then a freighter over ocean and cloud, also from overhead [verified, desktop-s75.png]; then white informational blocks and a white footer [verified, desktop-s100.png].

The signature is the **camera, not the sequence**: every ground beat is plan view, so the page reads like a dispatch console rather than a showreel. A `00 km/h` readout sits beside the road beat [verified, index.html `.home-service-speed-number`], which makes scroll velocity legible as freight velocity. Voice: operator-plain, service-promise led — "unified across APAC under one accountable team" [verified fragment, index.html meta description].

## 3. Visual language
Not a mono-hue dark system. The hero is the only full-dark screen; everything after it is white with black and photographic insets, and the token file defines light and dark variants of every surface, content and border role side by side [verified, webflow-shared.css]. Colour arrives three ways: the ultramarine `#0016cb` as link and brand accent (footer nav, map dots), the orange `#f50` as interaction accent (cursor, button hovers, cart and checkout), and the cargo itself — a teal reach-stacker, orange and blue containers, blue ocean — carried in the photography [verified, webflow-shared.css, desktop-s100.png, mobile-s75.png]. ## 4. Motion and effects (with parameters)
- Scroll layer on the home route alone: 40 ScrollTrigger instances, 56 `scrub` flags, 14 pins [verified, chunk-Home-CZ3JhV-m.js]. Ranges are written in viewport multiples — `top+=250vh → top+=440vh`, `top+=100vh → top+=450vh` — so single scenes own 200–350vh of scroll [verified, same].
- Every scrubbed tween is `ease:"none"` (60 uses), which is the correct discipline [verified, same]. Entrance easing is `power1.inOut` ×10, `circ.inOut` ×7, `sine.inOut` ×6, `back.out(1.3)` ×3, `expo.out` ×2; durations cluster at 1s (70), 1.2s (20), 0.5s (13) [verified, same].
- CSS motion is narrower: `cubic-bezier(.445,.05,.55,.95)` ×26 and `cubic-bezier(.22,1,.36,1)` ×4, with `.4s` the default transition (121 uses) [verified, webflow-shared.css].
- **The land beats are image sequences, not real-time 3D.** 554 `.avif` frame paths under `home-service/crane/{lift-up,rotate,put-container}` and `home-service/container-truck`, played into `<canvas class="home-service-crane-sq|truck-sq">` by a sequence player configured `{windowed:true, fit:"cover", concurrency:6, progressive:true, lerp:1, scrollTrigger:{…}}` with a `skipOdd` decimation pass [verified, chunk-Home-CZ3JhV-m.js, index.html].
- WebGL is reserved for three things: the hero globe (`createGlobe` on `<canvas id="globe">`, driven by a `globePhi` ticker, gated by an IntersectionObserver with `rootMargin:"100px"` and a ResizeObserver) [verified, chunk-Home-CZ3JhV-m.js, chunk-globe.js]; a `my-flights` arc module that imports the Three.js vendor chunk [verified, chunk-my-flights.js]; and an `OceanScene` with a `WakeSimulation` exposing `fov:35, wakeSpeed:50, wakeCenterY, wakeStartY, wakeWidth, wakeForce, wakeIntensity, height, overlayScale` [verified, chunk-Home-CZ3JhV-m.js, main.js chunk names].
- Page transitions are Barba-driven [verified, main.js `vendor-barba` chunk, `deferDestroyUntilAfterLeave` / `next.container` lifecycle in chunk-globe.js]; Lenis handles `scrollTo`, hash landing and `scrollRestoration:"manual"` [verified, chunk-scroll-CRhVCsWB.js].

## 5. Tech and pipeline
Webflow shell with `x-wf-template` collection markup behind the insights / articles / ai-news routes and Webflow Ecommerce on Stripe behind `/merchandises` and `/checkout` [verified, index.html]; the whole motion and 3D layer is one Rolldown bundle served from a separate Netlify origin and injected as a module script [verified, index.html + main.js]. Route chunks (`Home`, `Service`, `Industry`, `About`, `Article`, `Career`, `Community`, `Contact`, `Merchandise`, `Checkout`, `Payment`, `Terms`, `TpInsight`, `TpProduct`) and scene chunks (`OceanScene`, `WakeSimulation`, `globe`) are all dynamic imports, so the GL cost is deferred per beat [verified, main.js]. `main.js` itself is 50.5 KB, the home chunk 126.6 KB, the globe chunk 10.5 KB [verified, fetched sizes]. Fonts: five subset woff2 on the Webflow CDN, `font-display:swap` [verified, webflow-shared.css]. Images: 169 `<img>`, 164 `loading="lazy"`, 47 with `srcset`, one `<video>` [verified, index.html]. Six canvases on desktop, five on mobile, 3106 DOM nodes [verified, manifest.json]. Two identical page errors — `Cannot read properties of undefined (reading 'end')` on desktop and desktop-rm [verified, manifest.json]. The manifest's LCP figures are a headless SwiftShader cold-cache artefact and say nothing about real-world load; CLS is the usable number: 0.0004 desktop, 0.0007 reduced-motion, **0.34 on mobile** [verified, manifest.json].

## 6. Weaknesses
- **Reduced motion: fail.** Exactly one `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce` guard exists in the entire homepage bundle, on a single parallax effect (`.home-why-ship-img-inner`); the stylesheet and the served HTML contain none at all [verified, chunk-Home-CZ3JhV-m.js, webflow-shared.css, index.html]. `desktop-rm-s25` and `desktop-rm-s50` are byte-identical to their normal counterparts, and the other three differ only because the globe and clouds keep animating [verified, md5 over the capture set]. Nothing is left hidden, so the page stays readable — the preference is simply ignored.
- **Keyboard: fail.** No `keydown` / `keyup` / Escape handler anywhere in `main.js` or the home chunk; no `tabindex`, no `aria-expanded` on the menu toggle [verified, main.js, chunk-Home-CZ3JhV-m.js, index.html]. The long pinned sequences have no skip.
- **Landmarks: fail.** No `<main>`, no `<nav>`, no skip link in the served HTML; one `<header>`, one `<footer>`, ten `<section>`, seven `aria-label` [verified, index.html]. Awwwards' Accessibility 6.80 is the lowest of its six developer sub-scores [verified, Awwwards entry read 2026-09-18].
- **DOM behind the canvas: pass.** One H1, a real H2/H3 outline per section, carrier logos carry meaningful `alt` (AIR INDIA, CMA CGM, COSCO, Emirates…), and the whole page reads without the GL layer [verified, index.html].
- **The phone: pass on design, fail on stability.** The phone frame is composed, not shrunk — monogram logo, dotted MENU toggle, the globe re-cropped to Europe and Africa [verified, mobile-s00.png]. But mobile CLS is 0.34 and the page scrolls an inner container rather than the document, which breaks native scroll restoration and anchor behaviour [verified, manifest.json, chunk-scroll-CRhVCsWB.js].
- **Content: one visible defect.** A feature heading reads "GLOBAL NETWORK OVERAGE" where "COVERAGE" is meant [verified, desktop-s50.png]. Content scored 7.16 [verified, Awwwards entry read 2026-09-18].
- **Wayfinding.** No chapter indicator across 200–350vh scenes; the primary action is a hero pill and is not reachable again until the footer [verified, capture set].
- What the awards skills do differently: give every scene a heading and a skip target, put the sequence behind a real reduced-motion tier (static key frame per beat), keep the document as the scroll container on every breakpoint, add `<main>` and `<nav>` and an Escape path on the menu overlay, and hold mobile CLS under 0.1 by reserving the canvas box.

## 7. Principles
1. Map scroll to the literal operation the business performs; the process sequence is a free storyboard.
2. Fix one unusual camera and never break it — a consistent plan view does more identity work than a second accent colour.
3. A pre-rendered frame sequence buys photoreal motion at a fraction of the authoring cost of real-time 3D; spend the GL budget only where interaction or continuity demands it.
4. Put a live readout next to the motion (speed, distance, count) so the effect carries information, not just polish.
5. One expressive face, one silent face, one mono for the machine voice.
6. Give a boring category a genuine utility — a regional news feed, a calculator, a tracking portal — so the site is a destination, not a leaflet.
7. A dark hero can open a light site; the contrast between the two grounds is itself a chapter break.

## 8. Take / Don't take
- **Take:** scroll-as-process structure, 4–6 beats, each morphing into the next `[pattern:narrative-structures#chaptered-journey]`; the scrubbed frame-sequence technique with windowed progressive loading and a linear scrub ease `[recipe:scroll-pin-scrub]`; GL confined to the beats that need continuity `[recipe:gl-rtt-composite-transition]`; the telemetry readout tied to scroll velocity; the wordmark that collapses to a monogram on scroll; the two-token accent split (brand colour for identity, a second for interaction only); a bespoke icon set and a designed footer as deliverables; a living-utility route.
- **Don't take:** `#0016cb` + `#f50`, BT Steinhart, the orbit → crane → truck → ship → plane order, the plan-view aerials, the 12rem orange cursor, the merch and AI-news routes bolted on without a reason — and none of its accessibility posture: no landmarks, no keyboard path, no reduced-motion tier, an inner mobile scroll container.
