## 1. Concept and narrative
"Ocean Autonomy That Works" is an anti-hype claim in a category full of vapourware, and the product is reframed as "Satellites at sea" [verified fragments, index.html `<h1>`, `ProductIntro_heading`]. The spine is scale escalation: vessels "designed to work independently, team up for coordinated maneuvers, and form vast networks for ocean-wide intelligence" [verified fragment ≤ 25 words, `ProductIntro_copy`] — so scroll depth equals scope. The second beat inverts the sell: a six-item flythrough lists what the ocean hides rather than what the boat does — "The coordinates of the ghost fleet.", "The path of hurricanes.", "The signals of submarines." [verified fragments, `FlythroughPoints_pointText`]. Only after that does the page name a product. Specs are rhetoric, not tables: "Sprints 35+ knots. Cruise 400+ nm.", "Resilient to GPS jamming and spoofing, with anti-tamper capabilities available." [verified fragments, `desktop-s75.png`]. The recurring formula is capability stated as a constraint removed — no crew, no crane, no specialist training.

## 3. Visual language
The page is a three-act colour system, not a two-token palette. A fixed `#theme-background` stacks three gradient layers and drives `--opacity-aura / --opacity-oxidised / --opacity-copper` from scroll triggers, so the ground itself cross-fades between acts: pale mint-and-cream (aura) for the product chapters, sea-green into deep olive (oxidised) for missions and Heavyfish, amber into burnt maroon (copper) for Quickfish and the close [verified, index.html inline style + `css-*.css` + `desktop-s25/50/75.png`]. The greens and rusts are read off the operating environment — oxidised metal, marine hazard marking — and nothing is neon. Vessel renders sit on the gradient with no box, cut out and scaled to near-full-bleed; captioned photo and video cards float over them with `0.8rem` radius.

Type is a three-voice contract: a high-contrast serif for display and every heading, a neutral grotesque for body, a squarish techno mono for labels, CTAs, specs and footer meta [verified, `css-*.css`]. There is no `clamp()` anywhere. Instead `html{font-size:0.6944444444vw}` locks 1rem to 10px on a **1440 px artboard** and scales the entire scale with the viewport, with breakpoint swaps to `1.3020833333vw` (768) and `2.5445292621vw` (393) [verified, same]. Grid: `--columns:24` desktop / 8 mobile, `--gutter:1.2rem`, `--margin:3.2rem` / `1.6rem`, `--radius:0.8rem`, `--headerHeight:7.2rem` [verified]. A `--devGrid-columnColor:rgba(255,0,0,10%)` overlay ships in production CSS [verified]. Browser surfaces are handled by the two `browser-ui-overlay` strips rather than `theme-color`, which is absent [verified, index.html head].

## 4. Motion and effects (with parameters)
- **Scroll**: native, Lenis-smoothed; `data-lenis-prevent` only on the nav panel. Stage motion is scroll-progress-driven through custom properties (`--progress`, `--hero-scale`, `--hero-translateY:50%`, `--brief-translateX:-10%`, `--gallery-translateX:10%`, `--nav-frameProgress`) [verified, `css-*.css`].
- **Frame sequences**: `AnimateFrames` is a canvas painter, not `<img>` swapping — `_track` (the scroll length) → `_loadTrigger` (a lazy sentinel that starts the fetch) → `_container` → `_display` → `<canvas>`. Four instances: one flythrough, three vessel stories keyed by `data-asset` [verified, index.html]. Frame count, resolution and source paths [unknown] — no numbered frame URLs appear in the served HTML.
- **Theme cross-fade**: three stacked gradient layers, opacity per theme, switched by `data-theme-trigger` sentinels and `ThemeSwitch` sections between vessels [verified].
- **Text**: `AnimatedText` duplicates each label into `labelOriginal` (with `aria-label`) and an `aria-hidden` `labelCopy`, then rolls per character via `--char-index` / `--char-amt` [verified]. `InViewText` / `InViewElement` handle enter reveals.
- **Pointer**: `NavProximityOffsetter` blends hover into progress — `--currentProgress: calc(var(--progress,0) + (var(--hover) - var(--progress,0)) * var(--hoverImpact))` [verified].
- **Easing vocabulary**: `cubic-bezier(.215,.61,.355,1)` easeOutCubic is the house curve (17 uses), then easeInCubic `(.55,.055,.675,.19)` ×7, easeOutQuint `(.23,1,.32,1)` ×3, easeOutExpo `(.19,1,.22,1)` ×1. Durations cluster at .3s (29), .4s (22), .8s (17), .6s (16), .5s (12) [verified, `css-*.css`].
- **Footer telemetry**: scroll distance accumulated into metres and a rolling average into knots, then mapped to a simile band — the only place on the page where the interaction is the content.
- **Sound**: none in the markup [verified].

## 6. Weaknesses
- **Reduced motion**: `desktop-rm-s00/25/50/75/100.png` are byte-for-byte near-identical to the motion frames, so nothing is left stranded at opacity 0 — the page reads at rest. But **zero `prefers-reduced-motion` rules exist across all five stylesheets** [verified], so this is server-rendered content surviving, not an authored tier. The awards skills ship three explicit tiers.
- **Meta is broken**: `og:image` and `twitter:image` resolve to `https://www.seasats.comhttps//seasats.sfo3.cdn…/Cover-1200x630.jpg`, and the hero `<link rel="preload" as="video">` has the same doubled origin; `twitter:creator` is still the template's `@payloadcms` [all verified, index.html]. Semantics/SEO 7.00 and Markup/Meta 7.20 are the cheapest points on the board to have lost.
- **Accessibility 6.60 is the lowest developer sub-score** [verified, Awwwards]. Heading order is sound (1 h1, 10 h2, 23 h3, 24 h4) and the filters, tabs and x-ray toggle are real `<button>`/`<input>` elements, but 88 of 91 `<img>` carry `alt=""` [verified], and the globe is sold as "Click and drag" with no stated keyboard path [verified fragment; keyboard handling **unknown**].
- **Nav model differs by device**: hover-opened on desktop, button-opened on phone, with the pointer-proximity offset having no touch equivalent [verified, index.html + `mobile-s00.png`]. Usability 7.17 is the lowest jury axis.
- **A production dev-grid overlay** and an untree-shaken three.js core for one globe are both shipped weight [verified].
- **A blank capture frame**: `desktop-s25.png` and `desktop-rm-s25.png` are a bare oxidised gradient with only the wordmark. That scroll offset sits in the `missions` globe stage, whose intro is `InViewText`-gated and whose canvas is R3F — the frame is empty because the globe never painted under headless SwiftShader, not because the section is empty [frame emptiness verified, cause inferred]. The awards skills give every canvas stage a DOM mirror that survives a dead GL context.

## 7. Principles
1. Spend the weirdness budget on one element — here the vertical menu and its colour ladder — and keep the body clean.
2. Make the ground the chapter marker: cross-fade a small set of named full-bleed gradients so colour, not a label, tells the reader which act they are in.
3. Lock the type scale to a named artboard with a `vw`-derived root font size instead of per-element `clamp()`; the whole system then scales as one drawing.
4. Open on what the subject reveals about the world, not on the subject; name the product only once the stakes are set.
5. A canvas frame player with its own scroll track and a lazy load sentinel buys the "3D site" read with near-zero runtime risk — keep real WebGL for the one moment that must be interactive.
6. Put one piece of live state in the footer: an interaction that measures the visit is remembered longer than a section that describes the product.

## 8. Take / Don't take
- **Take:** the three-gradient theme background driven by scroll-triggered opacity tokens `[pattern:color-and-material]`; the artboard-locked `html{font-size:Xvw}` scale with breakpoint swaps; the vertical menu whose every item carries its own background and flips its ink `[pattern:components-catalog]`; scroll-tracked canvas frame sequences with a lazy load sentinel `[recipe:image-sequence-scrub]`; poster-first video slots; a native checkbox for an x-ray toggle instead of a custom control; real `<button>` filters with the active one `disabled`; roll-over text that keeps one labelled copy and hides the duplicate; scroll telemetry as footer content; the one-to-many-to-system escalation `[pattern:narrative-structures#specification]`; the easeOutCubic-dominant curve set with .3–.8s durations.
- **Don't take:** the aura / oxidised / copper gradients and the `#D7C5BA→#2B3922` menu ladder [verified, index.html]; "Ocean Autonomy That Works", "Satellites at sea" and the flythrough point lines; the vessel renders and cloudscapes; the section order; hover-only desktop menu opening; and do not ship the dev-grid overlay or a full three.js core for one mesh.
