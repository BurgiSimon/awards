## 1. Concept and narrative
- **The one idea:** merchants as modern Renaissance masters equipped with AI tools. Shopify chose the Renaissance because it "symbolizes progress, momentum, courage, and new beginnings" [recalled high — Shopify news via search] — apt for features that were not possible a year earlier.
- **How it unfolds:** a scroll-driven gallery. Each product update is anchored to its own 3D-rendered Renaissance painting; the changelog becomes a walk past the walls and the update copy is the label beside each canvas [recalled high]. Chapters follow product areas (AI/automation, online store, retail/POS, marketing, checkout, operations/analytics, developer platform) [recalled medium — third-party].
- **The metaphor reaches the motion layer:** transitions between chapters are brushstroke-edged — the wipe is painted, not geometric [recalled high — RGD].
- **Tone:** grand and art-historical in the visuals, plain and scannable in the update copy. The register gap is the charm — the same structural trick as [site:oryzo], inverted.
- **The series through-line is not a style but "a changelog deserves a concept":** every Edition invents a new world (generative paintings; the Boring Edition's anti-design toggle; "Everywhere") inside the same container [recalled high].

## 2. Structure and components
- **Header:** fixed, 50–60 px on desktop, carrying release identity, an edition selector to switch between past Editions, search, and a primary CTA [recalled medium-low — third-party].
- **Sidebar section nav:** visible on desktop, collapsible on mobile, numbered labels with connecting lines and an active state [recalled medium-low].
- **Mobile nav:** a full-height overlay of large stacked labels [recalled medium-low].
- **Sticky full-viewport scene layer** with static fallback media underneath, per section [recalled medium-low].
- **Hero:** release name, the launch idea, a one-line promise, the primary CTA and a visible cue that sections follow [recalled medium-low].
- **Update cards** with filtering and grouping, and a local search over the whole update set — keyboard-navigable, Escape to close [recalled medium-low].
- **404** treated as a designed moment in the same world, established in Winter '24 [recalled high — Awwwards inspiration].
- **Content schema observed:** `ReleaseSection { id, navLabel, eyebrow, title, summary, theme, scene, updates }` and `ProductUpdate { id, title, category, summary, tags, media, cta }` [recalled medium-low — third-party].

## 3. Visual language
- **Imagery:** generative, 3D-rendered Renaissance paintings with "rich textures, warm luminosity, dramatic composition" [recalled high — RGD / Awwwards via search], mashed up with modern-commerce elements; character animation inside the paintings, human-finished [recalled high].
- **Palette roles:** a black stage behind the scenes; paper and ink for the reading layer; muted panels for cards; one product accent per chapter [recalled medium-low]. Note the content black is warm (#292919) while the stage is pure #000000 — the batch-wide "no pure black as content black" rule holds, with the stage as the diegetic exception.
- **Type:** serif display + grotesque + a script for moments; the serif-display + grotesque pairing recurs in [site:floema], but here a third layer exists because 150 discrete items need more hierarchy than a craft site does. Negative tracking only at display size.
- **Layout:** the sticky scene fills the viewport; editorial content scrolls over it in chapters; the rail on the side indexes them [recalled medium-low].
- **Browser surfaces:** [unknown]; the third-party doc expects visible focus rings and contrast in both light and dark nav themes [recalled medium-low].

## 4. Motion and effects (with parameters)
- **Confirmed for this site [recalled high — RGD]:** brushstroke-edge transitions between sections; Theatre.js so designers could direct on-page motion directly; texture compression to hold 60 fps on mobile, where 70 % of sessions occurred.
- **The painting effect, as reconstructed by a third party [recalled medium-low]:** the paintings are 2.5D — a parallax occlusion mapping shader fed by a source JPG plus a grayscale depth map, ray-marched with a forward pass and a backward refinement pass to find the depth-surface intersection, giving real occlusion and mouse/scroll parallax with no geometry. Depth maps of this kind come from tools such as DepthAnything V2. On top: wave-mask scroll transitions (sine-wave layering, scattered metallic highlights along the reveal edge, wave position bound to scroll progress), an UnrealBloomPass glow, dust particles reacting to the cursor, video textures in at least one scene, and bespoke per-artwork effects (a travelling light band, a proximity-triggered electric arc). Treat the pass counts as one analyst's reconstruction; the image + depth-map approach is consistent with the look and with the "fine-tuned depth" wording in the RGD citation.
- **SIBLING — Spring '26 "Everywhere" engineering [recalled high for Spring '26 — Codrops via search; indicative for the family]:** one fullscreen WebGL canvas; on scroll the app resolves the active scene, mounts it plus its immediate neighbours and composites between them, unmounting and disposing everything else. Scroll position drives uniforms, not React renders — camera motion, section transitions, screen offset, point-cloud displacement and post-processing parameters are read from refs inside the render loop, so the DOM page and the scene stay locked. Volumetric light from video via KTX2 array textures rendered as raymarched boxes; cross-browser transparent video via a stacked RGB + alpha video and a WebGL2 shader; a custom point-cloud format with quantized positions and chroma subsampling; a shared fluid-field system driving cursor- and scroll-reactive effects across scenes; a four-tier GPU device system. Stated goal: "cinematic while remaining true to the web: responsive, accessible, performant, and usable across a wide range of devices".
- **Numbers known:** 60 fps target on mobile; 70 % mobile sessions [recalled high]; tier targets DPR ≤ 2 at high, 30 fps at low [recalled medium-low — third-party]. Easings, durations and staggers [unknown] — the third-party doc only notes "staggered entry animations".

## 5. Tech and pipeline
- **Stack:** Theatre.js + Three.js/WebGL with texture compression [recalled high]; Remix on Oxygen behind an iframe shell, Tailwind v4 [recalled medium-low]; SIBLING React + Three.js [recalled high for Spring '26].
- **Asset pipeline as documented by the third-party checklist — recommended for this class of site, not published by Shopify [recalled medium-low]:** `.glb` with Draco geometry compression and KTX2/Basis textures; `GLTFLoader`, `DRACOLoader`, `KTX2Loader.detectSupport(renderer)`, `RGBELoader`, loaders cached and reused; hero models under ~5 MB compressed, supporting assets under ~2 MB, textures 1024–2048 px; PMREM environments at 1K.
- **Quality tiers [recalled medium-low — third-party]:** High (60 fps, DPR ≤ 2, full effects) / Medium (DPR capped, fewer particles) / Low (30 fps target, minimal effects, fallback acceptable), chosen from WebGL support, DPR, viewport, `prefers-reduced-motion` and measured post-load FPS.
- **Degradation [recalled medium-low — third-party]:** full WebGL scene → static fallback image/video per section → plain text and card layout, with asset failures isolated to individual scenes so one broken asset cannot take down the page.
- **Performance posture:** mobile is the primary surface (70 %) and the compression work targeted 60 fps there [recalled high — RGD] — the most maximal site in batch C is mobile-first in its budget.

## 6. Weaknesses
- **Scores unknown** — the jury's axis breakdown for this edition could not be read; the Summer '24 sibling's 7.27 shows the series can win with a modest score.
- **The iframe shell** (if the third-party observation is right) complicates deep links, focus order, scroll restoration and skip links [inferred consequence].
- **150+ scenes** mean real asset weight and a taste risk: hybrid AI-foundation artwork can carry a uniform generated sheen unless the human finish is strong; 2.5D parallax also smears at depth edges under large parallax offsets [inferred].
- **Accessibility claims are second-hand:** skip links, keyboard navigation, focus rings and the text-only tier are documented by an outside analyst, not by Shopify [recalled medium-low].
- **Critique found:** none substantive about the craft; commentary (e.g. Series Eight) argued about the product claims [recalled medium].
- **What the awards skills do differently:** the plugin adopts this site's posture as its defaults — `/awards:webgl` tiers high / mid / low from WebGL support, DPR, viewport, `prefers-reduced-motion` and measured post-load FPS, per-scene failure isolation, a static poster under every scene; `/awards:motion` treats reduced motion as first-class (static painting, no wave transitions, no particles, hierarchy preserved). It refuses the iframe shell: one document, native anchors, a skip link and a walkable focus order. The audit flags the pure #000000 stage (rule C02) unless declared diegetic in `AWARDS.md ## Exceptions`. Assets with an AI substrate are labelled synthetic in the brief. The DOM mirror holds — every update card is real content under an `aria-hidden` canvas — and the load gates are scene-windowed: only the hero scene gates first paint, LCP from its static fallback ≤ 2.5 s, GL chunk lazy ≤ 500 KB gz.

## 7. Principles (3–6, generalisable)
1. **Give a boring content type a concept.** The content did not change; the frame did — 150 bullet points became 150 paintings.
2. **One metaphor, carried into the motion layer.** If the world is painted, the wipe between chapters is painted too.
3. **Depth without geometry.** Image + depth map + parallax occlusion gives a 3D-feeling scene at image-file cost, which is how 150 of them can run at 60 fps on phones.
4. **Put the motion controls in designers' hands.** Polish comes from iteration count; a timeline tool removes the engineer from the loop.
5. **Design the degradation, not just the maximum.** Three explicit tiers with per-scene isolation and a measured-FPS quality ladder.
6. **Mobile-first budgets on a maximalist site.** The ambition survived because the budget was real.
7. **Reinvent the concept every release; keep the container.** Same nav, edition switcher, cards and search — a new world each time makes a series, not a redesign treadmill.

## 8. Take / Don't take
- **Take:**
  - Scene-window mounting: mount the active section's scene plus its immediate neighbours, composite between them in one canvas, unmount and dispose the rest.
  - Scroll → ref → rAF, never scroll → state: one scroll number written to a ref and read in the render loop for camera, uniforms and post-processing.
  - Depth-map parallax backgrounds as a 2.5D technique tier: one JPG + one grayscale depth PNG per section, a ray-marched POM shader, mouse/scroll parallax, optional bloom and dust — reserved for sections that earn a scene.
  - Mask transitions whose reveal position is bound to scroll progress, with a treated edge (texture, flecks) instead of a hard line — in your own material, not brushstrokes.
  - Quality tiering with measured post-load FPS: DPR ≤ 2 at high, DPR capped at mid, 30 fps at low; drop particles and post-processing first. Three-tier content degradation with per-scene failure isolation.
  - Designer-directed motion through a timeline tool (Theatre.js or equivalent) when a page has dozens of authored moments.
  - Content schema before scenes: a `Section { id, navLabel, eyebrow, title, summary, theme, scene, items }` shape rendered from data, then the GL layer on top.
  - An edition/version switcher and a ⌘K-style local search (keyboard-navigable, Escape to close) whenever a page carries 100+ items; a numbered rail with connecting lines and active state; an authored 404; a mobile-first frame budget (60 fps on the majority device).
- **Don't take:**
  - The Renaissance / old-masters world, "The Renaissance Edition" or "RenAIssance" naming, the painting-per-update device, or brushstroke wipes as a skin.
  - #000000 stage + #f7f7ee paper + #292919 ink + #739bff focus, or the serif + grotesque + script trio as-is.
  - The seven-chapter product taxonomy, the header/sidebar composition, or the Boring Edition toggle gag.
  - Shopify's artworks, videos, character animations or launch copy.
