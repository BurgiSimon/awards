## Brief
- **Subject:** `awards`, a Claude Code and Codex plugin (MIT, by BurgiSimon; version 1.0.0 read from its manifest at build) that designs and builds award-level websites and components end to end: brief, concept, visual system, structure, stack, motion, WebGL, a forked jury that scores the page the way an award jury does, and ship. This site is its public page (`site/` in the plugin's repository), redesigned for the 1.0.0 release. The baseline is the "Contact sheet" build (shipped 2026-10-23; its record is `.awards/redesign-2/baseline-AWARDS.md` and `baseline-DESIGN.md`).
- **Unique mechanism (what only this subject can prove):** the plugin does not trust its own work. Every build is held to a written contract in `AWARDS.md` and handed, as captures, to a juror in a separate context that never saw it built; the juror scores Design 40 · Usability 30 · Creativity 20 · Content 10 about 0.7 below the published award on purpose and ends on one of four words, usually `fix` first. The records of real builds prove it: the Museum of Extinct Colours' first-viewport pass came back `rebuild` (Usability 5.5) before it shipped at 7.8; the Petrel cable-ship build stopped at `fix` 7.26 with Usability 6. And 1.0.0 dates the plugin's own rut: `house-tells.md` read its five finished builds as "one studio's formula".
- **Inventory (eight to twelve things that belong to this client and no competitor, separated by semicolons: the name and what it means, the place, the numbers, the one constraint, materials, rituals, printed things, people):** the four words a juror ends on, `ship`, `fix`, `rebuild`, `recapture` (bound from `agents/awards-jury.md`); `AWARDS.md`, the file every phase writes and the next one reads, with its Jury log of dated one-line verdicts; the four showcase builds and their real jury trails (`showcase/ekvator`, `extinct`, `petrel`, `awards-home`; fictional clients labelled synthetic in their own briefs); the calibration: this jury scores about 0.7 below the published award, by design; 40 / 30 / 20 / 10, the Awwwards weights printed on every entry; the memory test, "an object or a behaviour, never a mood"; 95 audit rules in eight families, the slop scan that caps Creativity at 6 at two families; 65 award sites read in depth, every fact labelled verified, recalled, inferred or unknown; 96 recipes, each with its own frame shot in headless Chromium (`recipes/_gallery/frames/`); the seeded roll that deals three directions from seven worlds, 14 engines and 44 outside stimuli; the house ledger, in which the plugin names its own spent shapes; two commands per client, Node 22.12+ or 24 LTS.
- **Audience and their real scene:** designers and front-end developers who already use Claude Code or Codex. They arrive from the GitHub repository or the plugin marketplace, on a laptop with a terminal beside the browser (a phone second, from a shared link), with under a minute before deciding to install or close the tab. They have asked a model for "a nice website" before and know what comes back. A second visit is a lookup: a command, a recipe, a rule.
- **Stakes and conversion (what a visitor should do, matched to the price of the decision):** free, MIT, two commands, reversible. In order: (1) install: copy both clients' commands, read from the repository README at build, never typed by hand; (2) read how a build goes; (3) open the recipes.
- **Assets on hand:** owned, all real: the four showcase builds (their pages, captured by this build with `capture.mjs`; their `AWARDS.md`, `.awards/jury/` and `.awards/ship/` reports, quoted verbatim with file and date); the recipe explorer's 96 frames in `../plugins/awards/recipes/_gallery/frames/` (800 × 500 WebP, MIT, the plugin's own); the site cards in `../plugins/awards/references/sites/`; the plugin's changelog, READMEs and data files. No photographs, video or 3D of people or places. No testimonials, users, stars or download numbers exist and none are invented. Synthetic: anything drawn for this build (marks, diagrams) is labelled so; the showcase clients (Ekvator, the Museum of Extinct Colours, C/S Petrel) are fictional and the page says so.
- **Binding constraints:** static Vite multi-page build, relative `base`, deployed by `../.github/workflows/site.yml` unchanged (`npm ci`, `npm run build` into `dist/`, `SITE_ROOT`, `SITE_URL`); every count, the version, the commands and the rule tables read from `../plugins/awards` at build (`facts/`); WCAG 2.2 AA; no analytics and no runtime requests to other origins; no invented testimonials, users or numbers. The spent shapes of `../docs/handoff/house-ledger.md` and `house-tells.md` wave 1 are not repeated: the page as an object or a capture of itself, a readout in the margin, the scroll as a physical process, code-only imagery, a light restrained duotone. Edit only `site/`; the plugin is read-only; no git operations by this build (the redesign skill's branch step is skipped by instruction). `programs: awwwards`, target Site of the Day.
- **Routes:** 5 — home (the redesign), install, docs, recipes (96 entries with frames), 404; the inner routes keep their content and take the new world.
- **Sound:** none.
- **Visitor mode:** persuade (install is the conversion); /install, /docs and /recipes are read.
- **References the user likes:** none.
- **Calibration:** can win on Creativity and Content (real verdicts on real builds, quoted with file and date: material no competitor holds); most likely to lose on Usability if the signature delays or hides the install commands; the usability walk checks first that both clients' commands are copyable in the first viewport at 390 px and by keyboard.
