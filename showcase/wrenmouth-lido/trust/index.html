£4.2 million, mostly from here.

Six years of restoration cost £4.2 million. 61 % of it came from members and local people: subscriptions, a share offer, sponsored swims in the harbour, two auctions and a great many cake stalls. The rest came from heritage grants.

How it went.

  1. 1937

    The council opens the lido on the sea wall: 60 metres of seawater, a stepped cascade fountain at the shallow end, a diving stage at the deep end, and a turnstile hall to pay your tuppence at.

  2. 1979

    The diving stage is taken down after a winter storm cracks its footings. The deep end isn't deep enough for one under today's rules, so it stays down.

  3. 1991

    Repairs to the sluice are priced at more than the council can find. The lido closes at the end of the summer.

  4. 1991–2019

    Twenty-eight years empty. The sea still comes over the wall twice a month. Every few years somebody proposes flats.

  5. 2019

    The Wrenmouth Lido Trust is set up in the back room of the Anchor, and buys the lido from the council for £1, with a promise to reopen it.

  6. 2019–2025

    £4.2 million raised. The tank relined, the sluice gate rebuilt, the cascade working again, the turnstile hall made into a café, a sauna built on the wall.

  7. 2027

    Open at 06:00 on Saturday 14 June, then every day of the year.
