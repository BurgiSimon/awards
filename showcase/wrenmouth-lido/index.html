Twice a month the sea comes in.

The Wrenmouth Lido isn't filled from a tap. On the spring tides, around each new moon and full moon, the sea comes over the sluice in the wall and refills all 60 metres of it.

Nobody heats it. That's the point. What's in the pool is what's in the bay, give or take a few days of sun: 9 °C in March, 19 °C in August, and every number in between on the board by the turnstile each morning.

The whole year in one table

Twenty-eight years empty.

For most of the time it's been standing there, the Wrenmouth Lido has been shut. Here's the short version.

  1. 1937

    The lido opens on the sea wall: a 60-metre pool, a stepped cascade fountain at the shallow end and a diving stage at the deep end. The tide fills it.

  2. 1979

    The diving stage comes down after a storm cracks its footings. It isn't coming back: the deep end isn't deep enough for one under today's rules.

  3. 1991

    The council closes it. The gates are chained and the sea keeps coming over the sluice to an empty tank.

  4. 2019

    The Wrenmouth Lido Trust buys it from the council for £1. Then the town raises £4.2 million, 61 % of it from members and local people.

  5. 2027

    Eight years after the pound, and two after the last of the restoration, it opens at 06:00 on 14 June. The Trust has 2,400 members and counting.

How the town saved it

What's on the wall.

A wood-fired sauna on the sea wall

Twelve seats, a window on the bay and a stove that takes an hour to get going. Sauna sessions are 40 minutes and booked at the turnstile.

The café in the old turnstile hall

The 1937 turnstiles are still there; the till is where the ticket office was. Tea, bacon rolls, soup in winter, and a window onto the shallow end so you can watch the kids.

Lifeguards on every session, and a swim school

Nobody swims without a lifeguard on the side, summer or winter. The swim school teaches children from four, and adults who never learned, in the shallow end.
