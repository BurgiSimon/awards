## Brief
- **Subject:** The Wrenmouth Lido — a 1937 seawater lido on the sea wall of Wrenmouth, a small harbour town on a cold northern coast; closed 1991, empty 28 years, bought by the Wrenmouth Lido Trust from the council for £1 in 2019 and restored over six years; reopens Saturday 14 June 2027 at 06:00 with a dawn swim, then open all year. Fictional client; the footer says so.
- **Unique mechanism (what only this subject can prove):** the pool is filled by the sea, not a tap — on the spring tides, twice a month, the tide comes over the sluice and renews 60 metres of water; the water is never heated, so the temperature on the day is the honest number (9 °C in March, 19 °C in August). No leisure centre and no spa can claim either.
- **Inventory (eight to twelve things that belong to this client and no competitor, separated by semicolons: the name and what it means, the place, the numbers, the one constraint, materials, rituals, printed things, people):** the name Wrenmouth and the sea wall it sits on; 1937, the year it opened; a 60-metre pool filled by the tide twice a month (the spring tides); the stepped cascade fountain at the shallow end; the diving stage, taken down in 1979 — its absence; 1991 to 2019, 28 years empty; bought for £1; 2,400 members of the Trust; £4.2 million raised, 61 % from members and local people; Saturday 14 June 2027, 06:00, the dawn swim, 400 places by ballot; water never heated, 9 °C March to 19 °C August; the wood-fired sauna on the sea wall and the café in the old turnstile hall; the swim school and lifeguards on every session.
- **Audience and their real scene:** four groups, read as one town: people in Wrenmouth and the county, on a phone, often reading it because someone in the family sent the link; cold-water swimmers who travel for a pool, comparing water temperature and opening hours, usually on a phone the evening before; families planning a summer day, checking prices and what to bring; summer visitors who found it from the harbour. The real scene is a kitchen table or a bus seat with a phone, not a studio monitor.
- **Stakes and conversion (what a visitor should do, matched to the price of the decision):** in this order — (1) enter the ballot for the opening dawn swim (free, 400 places; low price, high emotion: one tap from the first viewport); (2) buy a season pass (£145 adult, £60 under-16s, £95 concessions; a considered household decision: needs the facts — temperatures, hours, what's included); (3) join the Trust (£12 a year; a belonging decision: needs the story — the £1, the 28 years, the 2,400). No real payment is taken: forms are front-end only and say so.
- **Assets on hand:** none of our own. Imagery strategy: real public-domain / openly licensed photographs of the lido and sea-bathing world from open collections (Wikimedia Commons, Library of Congress, national archives, museum open-access) — each credited in a picture-credits list and in captions, and never presented as Wrenmouth's pool, town or members. Synthetic (authored for the build): all copy; the Trust's figures beyond those in the brief (none invented — only brief numbers used); the tide dates (computed approximation, labelled as illustrative); the form flows (front-end only, no backend); the wordmark (a typeset name, not a logo). **Synthetic facts written for the build (not in the client brief), all plausible and none presented as verified:** month-by-month water temperatures other than 9 °C (March) and 19 °C (August) (`src/data/months.json`); first-swim durations and what-to-wear advice (a rule of thumb, stated as such); opening hours (06:30–20:00 May–Sept, 07:00–17:00 Oct–Apr, 25 Dec 10:00–12:00); single-swim prices (£6 / £3 / £4) and sauna price (£5, £3 with a pass); refill-morning closures (~4 hours, twice a month); ballot dates (close 2 May 2027, public draw 8 May, returns by 24 May, redraw 25 May), the 100-place Wrenmouth-postcode share, gates at 05:15, photograph at 06:20, 12 + 2 lifeguards, one-person-plus-one-child entries; the 1979 storm and the "deep end not deep enough" reason; the 1991 sluice-repair reason; the Anchor back room; the restoration works list; Trust benefits (vote at the October AGM, members' board, *The Sluice* newsletter); pass rules (named, 14 June 2027–13 June 2028, early lanes, concession eligibility); travel details (station 12 min, bus 41, 120-space car park, 60 bike racks, hoist and accessible changing); the fictional address "The Sea Wall, Wrenmouth" and the placeholder domain `wrenmouthlido.example`; the derived money split (61 % of £4.2 m ≈ £2.56 m; 39 % ≈ £1.64 m — arithmetic, not new data).

## Direction contract
- **THESIS:** The first photograph of the restored Wrenmouth Lido has not been taken yet. It will be taken at 06:00 on Saturday 14 June 2027, and the 400 people drawn in the ballot will be in it. The site is built around that **empty frame**: every other picture on it is somebody else's pool or sea, captioned plainly with where it really is ("Not Wrenmouth."), and the one frame that is empty is ours — the visitor puts their name on the back of it by entering the ballot (engine `visitor-cast`, built on the inventory items *14 June 2027, 06:00, 400 places* and the brief's constraint *no photographs of our own*). Refused, as they would look here: **category rut** — the Art Deco lido-poster pastiche (a flat illustrated swimmer in a bathing cap, teal-and-coral sunburst, "Bathe at Wrenmouth" in deco capitals) and its spa-brochure twin (a drone shot of turquoise water at golden hour, "Reconnect with nature", a wellness icon grid, "Book now"); **Awwwards rut** — a near-black sea with a WebGL caustics/ripple shader under the cursor, a giant grotesque "DIVE IN", a marquee of "SWIM · SAUNA · CAFÉ", bubbles as a cursor trail, every section fading up; **template rut** — wordmark left and five links right, "EST. 1937" label over a two-line "Reopening 2027" headline bottom-left over a pool photo, a subline, then "Book now" filled + "Learn more" outline; **plugin rut** (wave 1 of `house-tells.md`) — "the page is a tide table / thermometer", the scroll filling the pool with a water level in the margin (refused: no readout), a pool drawn in code instead of a search of the collections, a light restrained duotone. This contract keeps none of the wave-1 devices: the frame is one object on the page, not the page; temperatures are written in sentences and in one table (no readout anywhere); the pictures are found, not drawn; the colour strategy was dealt (`theme-per-section`). One shared spent feature is kept on purpose — a light-dominant ground (house ledger: 3 of 5) — because the readers are on phones in daylight and the found photographs are mostly pale, sun-bleached prints; the dawn chapter supplies the one dark field, so the page is light by scene, not by habit. Competitor test: Tinside Lido, Plymouth — its first photograph was taken in 1935 and thousands since; it has no reopening dawn, no 400-place ballot and no empty frame, so the thesis does not survive the name swap. - **WORLD:** Colour strategy **theme-per-section** (dealt with `roll.mjs --choose committed-accent,theme-per-section,restrained-duotone --avoid restrained-duotone`, CHOSEN theme-per-section): the grounds are the lido's own surfaces and the sea's time of day — sea-wall concrete by daylight, the cold sea at dawn, the pale distemper of a painted pool — two values live at a time, swapped by chapter, with one action colour taken from the lifeguard's flag and spent only on the three conversions. Light-dominant, because the visitor reads it on a phone in daylight at a kitchen table or on a bus, and the dawn chapter is the one dark field (06:00 in June on a northern coast is a blue-grey half-light, not night). Four tokens at most (ground, ink, sea, flag); `awards:system` chooses hexes and faces. Type contract: one characterful text-and-display serif or grotesque with the plain, municipal directness of a 1930s town notice (not Art Deco display capitals), plus one neutral text face for forms and tables; captions as the second voice, set small like an archive caption.
