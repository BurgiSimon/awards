It follows the sun it borrows.

  • The angle

    A light bar travels behind the opal front and tilts, so a chair, a plant or the cup on your table throws its shadow where the borrowed sun would put it.

  • The warmth

    1,536 LEDs mix from 1,800 K at first light to 5,600 K under a high noon, and back down to the red of an evening, at the minute it happens in that city.

  • The length of day

    Lisbon gives you 10 h 54 min of light today. Nairobi gives you about 12 hours on every day of the year. Tromsø gives you 7 h 58 min today, and nothing at all in December.

Noon, taken apart.

  1. The travelling bar

    A 58 cm bar of 96 high-output LEDs rides a belt-driven rail and tilts ±40°, throwing hard light through the opal front at the borrowed sun's angle.

  2. The matrix

    1,536 LEDs in two whites and an amber, mixed every frame from 1,800 to 6,500 K at CRI 97: the soft sky around the hard light.

  3. The ephemeris

    Solar positions for 2,000 cities until the year 2100, computed on the panel itself. No account, no app, no cloud: you choose a city with the dial on its edge.
