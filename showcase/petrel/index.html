Fault desk

The internet is broken 4,212 metres down.

At 03:14 UTC the light in Gannet-1 stopped 2,147.3 km out from the shore station at Sines. Your own light crosses the sea to the other side in about 32 ms, one way, when the glass is whole.

Report a cable fault Tickets answer within 15 minutes, day or night.

Found with a pulse of light.

The echo
The shore station fires a pulse into the dead fibre and times the echo from the break. Glass slows light to about 204,000 km a second, five microseconds a kilometre: the echo came back after 21.47 ms, so the glass ends 2,147.3 km out.
The passage
The Petrel sailed from Ponta Delgada at 05:40 UTC with 18 km of spare cable coiled in her tanks. The position is 403 nautical miles away: 31 hours at 13 knots.
The cause
Most cables are broken by people: trawl nets and dragged anchors cause most of the 150 to 200 faults a year worldwide. At this depth it is rarer; here it was probably the seabed itself moving.

Four kilometres of wire, two hours down.

The grapnel, a forged hook of about 300 kg, goes over the bow on a steel wire paid out at 35 metres a minute. From here the readout at the top is the only way to know where it is.

Dragging blind.

The ship steams at about one knot across the cable's line, the grapnel on the bottom with one and a half times the depth of wire out. Nobody can see it. The only signal is the tension on the wire: flat, flat, flat, then a jump when the hook takes the cable.

Caught at 17.4 kN, 640 m from where the drag began.

Mended by hand, fibre by fibre.

Both ends come up through 4,212 m of water to the deck. Each is cut back to good glass; a new length is spliced in at two joints, in a clean room behind the bridge.

Per fibre
Aligned under a microscope and fused with an electric arc: under a minute.
Per joint
Sixteen fibres, the copper, the steel and the sheath rebuilt in a mould: most of a day.

Laid back on the bottom, eleven days later.

The mended cable is longer than the old one by about twice the depth, so it cannot go back in a straight line. It is lowered as a loop, a bight 8.4 km long, and laid on the seabed beside the old route.

Light through Gannet-1 again at 22:40 UTC, eleven days after it stopped.
