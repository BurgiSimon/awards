Hour 0 · Gather at 1,100 °C

Ines gathers from the furnace: a honey-thick glob of soda-lime glass on the end of a blowpipe, too bright to look at for long. Pieter waits at the lathe with the jacks already warm.

Varn fires once a day. One commission goes into the annealing oven each evening, so every record we keep belongs to one piece and one client.

Hours 0 to 1 · Working

The gather goes onto the glass lathe and turns at about forty revolutions a minute while Pieter shapes it with steel jacks and wet cherrywood paddles. Every pass takes heat out; within the hour the glass falls from 1,050 °C to about 600 °C.

There is no mould. Each profile is cut by hand against a drawing the client has signed, so no two pieces in a series share an exact line, and each one is logged under its own run number.

Hour 1 · The Draper point, 525 °C

From here you can no longer see that it is hot.

Every solid stops giving off visible light at about 525 °C. The vessel reads as clear glass for the first time, and it would still burn through a glove.

Hours 1 to 3 · The hold at 520 °C

Glass that cools unevenly keeps the difference as stress: the skin sets while the core is still shrinking, and the piece can crack weeks later on a shelf. Held at 520 °C, just under softening, the whole wall reaches one temperature and the stress relaxes out.

Hours 3 to 14 · The slow ramp

From the hold the oven comes down about 25 °C an hour to the strain point at 470 °C, then faster. Eleven hours with nothing to see, which is when the numbers matter.
